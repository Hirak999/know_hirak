Probing LoRA-to-LoRA Cross-Lingual Transfer for Unseen Low-Resource Conditions in Whisper-Based ASR (Interspeech 2026)

Hirak Mondal, Spandan Dey, Gopal Agrawal

Low-resource Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) remains a challenge despite large-scale pretraining, especially for languages with minimal representation. We investigate LoRA-to-LoRA transfer using Whisper, where a Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) module trained on a donor language, even one under-represented in the base model, initializes adaptation for a low-resource recipient. Unlike full fine-tuning, this approach preserves pretrained parameters, ensuring stable, parameter-efficient cross-lingual transfer. We propose a two-stage donor selection strategy that filters candidates by genealogical relatedness and identifies the optimal donor through orthographic-distributional similarity. Results demonstrate that donor-informed initialization consistently outperforms recipient-only adaptation, yielding relative Word Error Rate (WER) reductions of up to 17%. Our findings indicate that leveraging linguistically and orthographically aligned donors is critical for effective low-resource ASR.

Teacher-Free Knowledge Distillation for Improving Short-Utterance Spoken Language Identification (Interspeech 2025)

Spandan Dey, Hirak Mondal, Sanjay Kumar Kurmi

Spoken language identification (LID) systems exhibit performance degradations as the test input duration reduces. To delve deeper, we show that 36.94% of the misclassifications on 2-second (s) LID inputs occur due to out-of-scope elements like non-speech, named entities, filler words, and overlapped speech. To mitigate this, we propose a teacher-free knowledge distillation (TF-KD) using online label smoothing. This method accumulates prediction logits of correctly classified training segments from the preceding epoch and uses them as soft-labels for distillation in the next epoch. We further enhance TF-KD with dynamic weights, conditional label update, and entropy-based soft-label computation. Compared to existing KD-based solutions for 2s inputs, our approach achieves consistent Cavg improvements for both same-corpora and cross-corpora evaluations without training a separate teacher network.

Rethinking Organization Entity Modeling in End-to-End Acoustic Named Entity Recognition (Interspeech 2026)

Spandan Dey, Hirak Mondal, et.al.

End-to-end acoustic named entity recognition (NER) integrates speech transcription and entity detection within a unified model, avoiding error propagation in cascaded ASR-NER systems. However, organization entities remain significantly harder to detect due to multi-word spans, acronyms, and lexical variability. We present a systematic analysis of organization recognition failures in acoustic NER, revealing that conventional cross-entropy training inadequately models entity span dependencies. To address this limitation, we propose an organization-aware Whisper-based acoustic NER framework combining LLM-based targeted semantic augmentation, category-specific boundary supervision, and a structure-constrained entity learning (SCEL) objective that enforces entity span consistency during decoding. Experiments demonstrate substantial improvements in organization recognition while maintaining strong ASR performance, outperforming in entity-recognition for several other existing approaches.

Spoken Language Diarization using Source-Domain Aggregative Weakly-Supervised Language Labels (SPCOM 2026)

Spandan Dey, Hirak Mondal, et.al.

Spoken language diarization (SLD) detects homogeneous language segments in utterances containing code-switching or code-mixing. Although SLD is influenced by speaker diarization (SD), unlike speaker labels, language labels belong to a finite and defined set, enabling more structured modeling. Leveraging this, we propose to improve SLD by developing a domain-specific spoken language identification (LID) front-end directly from source-domain development data, where only RTTM annotations are available. Focusing on Track-2 of the DISPLACE 2024 challenge, we introduce an aggregative RTTM to pseudo-language label mapping (ARPM) framework using the Whisper-large-v3 model. ARPM generates weakly supervised language labels by aggregating language change tags from RTTM ground-truths, filtering them with duration and entropy-based criteria, and then refining the labels using pivot language based refinement. The resulting LID model produces more reliable domain-specific language-aware representations for subsequent diarization stages. The proposed approach achieves a 4.58% absolute improvement in diarization error rate (DER) over the baseline. Despite using only 20 hours of development data and a standalone framework, we achieve competitive performance with top-performing submissions to the challenge, leveraging thousands of hours of training data and system fusion.
